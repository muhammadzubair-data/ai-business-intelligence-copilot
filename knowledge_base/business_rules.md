# Business Rules and Calculation Policies

## Revenue recognition (Finance)
Finance recognises revenue when an order ships. Net revenue equals gross sales minus discounts minus refunds. Cancelled orders and orders not yet shipped are excluded. Refunds are attributed to the period of the original sale, not the date the refund was issued, so a month's net revenue can change slightly after it closes as late returns arrive.

## Booked revenue (Sales dashboard)
The Sales dashboard reports booked revenue: gross sales minus discounts on every non-cancelled order, dated by the order date, before refunds. Sales teams are measured on bookings because it reflects when the customer committed.

## Why Finance revenue and the Sales dashboard differ
The two numbers use different definitions and different dates. Booked revenue is by order date and ignores refunds; net revenue is by ship date and subtracts refunds. The gap is largest in months with high returns, at month-end when many orders are placed but not yet shipped, and at year end. Neither number is wrong; board reporting uses net revenue.

## Fiscal calendar
Halden's fiscal year starts on 1 February and is named after the calendar year in which it ends. FY2025 therefore runs from 1 February 2024 to 31 January 2025, and FY2026 runs from 1 February 2025 to 31 January 2026. Fiscal Q1 is February to April. When someone says "last year" without saying fiscal, analysts should use the calendar year and state that assumption.

## Active customers and churn
A customer is active if they placed at least one non-cancelled order within their activity window before the measurement date. Windows differ by segment because buying cycles differ: 90 days for Enterprise and Mid-Market, 180 days for SMB and 365 days for Consumer. A customer who was active at the start of a period and is no longer active at the end has churned in that period.

## Discount approval policy
Standard discounts are up to 10 percent for Enterprise, 8 percent for Mid-Market and 5 percent for SMB. Any line discount above 15 percent requires approval from the regional sales director, and discounts above 25 percent require CFO approval. Seasonal promotions in November and December are pre-approved for SMB and Consumer.

## Returns policy
Customers may return products within 30 days of delivery. Defective products are refunded in full and are not restocked. Other returns are restocked when the packaging is intact. Return rate is measured as refunds divided by sales after discounts.

## Customer acquisition cost
CAC is paid marketing spend divided by new customers acquired through paid channels (Paid Search, Paid Social, Email, Events, Affiliate). Organic, referral and direct-sales acquisitions are excluded from both sides of the ratio. Spend is attributed to the segment and region the campaign targets.

## Gross margin and contribution margin
Gross margin is gross profit (net revenue minus cost of goods sold) divided by net revenue. Cost of goods sold uses the supplier unit cost in effect on the order date. Contribution margin additionally subtracts channel fees, so it is the better measure when comparing channels.

## Operating profit and EBITDA
Operating profit is gross profit minus operating expenses. Depreciation and amortisation are booked inside the Other Operating expense type and are not reported separately, so EBITDA cannot be calculated from the warehouse. Requests for EBITDA should be answered with operating profit and a note explaining the difference.

## Web analytics data
Web sessions and web orders come from the web analytics tool, not the order system. Web orders are attributed by the analytics tag and will not reconcile exactly with orders in the order system. For revenue and order counts, use the order system.
